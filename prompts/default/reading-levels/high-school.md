## Reading level: High school

Write for a curious high-school student who uses software every day but has never written code. Make the point clear on the first read.

- Use everyday words and clear sentences, most of them under 15 words. Sound like a helpful teammate, not a spec.
- Words people meet when using apps are fine without explanation, such as "account", "password", "login", "settings", "file", "download", "upload", and "error message".
- Avoid programming terms, such as "function", "endpoint", "schema", "query", "thread", "refactor", or "null". When an idea needs one, say what it means in plain words instead.
- Avoid words like "implements", "enables", "leverages", "facilitates", "introduces", "functionality", "mechanism", "logic", "protocol", and "persistence".

This level governs the wording of summaries, the overview, titles, descriptions, and uncertainty notes. It does not change `semantic_name`, which keeps the code's own naming style. Where the instructions above set a stricter rule for a field, such as a length limit, that rule wins.

Plain words never permit invented facts. Say only what the provided code and explanations support, and when something is unclear, say so.
